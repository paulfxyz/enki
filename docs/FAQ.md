<h1 align="center">FAQ</h1>

<p align="center"><em>Short answers to the questions we get most.</em></p>

---

### Is Enki a company?

No. Enki is a public-good, non-profit, non-government association. It sells nothing, has no shares, and its financing is purely external — donations and the Advisory's consultancy revenue fund the public-good work.

### Is it legally registered?

Deliberately not yet. Where the association incorporates, and under what form, is a decision for the 300 — decided by the 300, not before. ([Governance →](GOVERNANCE.md))

### Does membership cost anything?

Nothing. There is no fee and no payment — a seat cannot be bought. Applicants are selected on what they can contribute: skills, networks or capital/donations.

### Why only 300 members?

299 individuals plus the founder — small enough that every member is known and reputation still works, large enough to carry real research, engineering, policy and capital work. An annual mutual audit keeps the circle at 300: the 10% judged to have contributed least leave, and 30 recruits step up.

### Can my company join?

No. Membership is for individuals only — no companies, no funds, no proxies. Each member is ID-verified and interviewed, confirmed one by one.

### Is Wally available?

Not yet — Wally is the ambition the 300 are being assembled to build: a fully open-source LibreChat fork with an Ollama-sourced engine, and a mesh layer that turns the devices you already own into your local AI datacenter. ([The plan →](WALLY.md))

### Is Enki against big AI models?

No — the manifesto argues for a **silo**: very-high-performance closed models confined to declared datacenters, rented under serious compliance; small open models everywhere else, local and effectively free, handling the vast majority of what people actually need. The frontier stays fenced; the commons stays open. ([Manifesto, Article IX →](../MANIFESTO.md))

### Why "effectively free" and not "free"?

Because nothing is free. The cost of everyday intelligence is the open-source model itself — either rented as tokens from a datacenter host or self-hosted on hardware you already own. That cost is collapsing; the registry documents it. ([The Registry →](REGISTRY.md))

### How do I apply?

At [enki.ngo](https://enki.ngo) — the application takes minutes. Every application is read by a person; whether your turn comes early or much later in the rolling cycle, you should count on an answer from us in your inbox one day.

### Can I use the manifesto, the texts, the images?

Yes — content is CC BY-SA 4.0 and the code is MIT. Share it, translate it, argue with it — with attribution, and keep it open. ([Licence →](../LICENSE.md))
