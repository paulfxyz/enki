# Two separate domains, progress shared

We propose neither locking advanced research away forever nor releasing untested models. Frontier datacenter systems and everyday AI would be kept separate and linked by a gradual, evidence-based path.

## Domain 1: frontier research and datacenters

Frontier research and datacenter-intensive computing, including any future superintelligence. The strictest containment, incident reporting and independent audits would follow dangerous capabilities; ordinary cloud research and industry would face proportionate rules.

## Domain 2: everyday AI

Assistance running on user-controlled devices or trusted local networks, designed for low running cost, open interfaces and good quality across languages. Small size, low cost or local hosting does not make a model safe; it still needs testing.

## A five-step path to wider availability

## Define the use and its risks

Set out the intended task, where it would be used, its effects on people’s rights and how it could be misused.

## Adapt and test

Where useful, adapt a capability, for example through distillation; such methods are options, not requirements. Test the model, its tools and its permissions. Do not assume a smaller model is safer.

## Independent review

Several independent assessors review the methods and results, with conflicts of interest disclosed. Public authorities approve only where the law requires it, not every everyday feature.

## Small-scale rollout

Start with a limited group. Measure real cost, energy use, hardware needs, interface compatibility and performance across languages and accessibility needs.

## Monitor and revise

Track incidents and feedback; pause or revise when needed. Service updates can be rolled back where feasible, but public weights and copies cannot reliably be recalled, and earlier harm cannot be undone.

## Resilience and open standards

Published methods and results, several providers and assessors, open interfaces and portable user context aim to keep everyday tools usable if one provider fails. They cannot remove network failures or security limits.

## The proposed Enki Institute

The Institute does not exist yet. We propose a research body with no regulatory power and no role as sole gatekeeper. It would publish its methods, results and conflicts of interest, including failures, for public authorities and citizens to use. It would not certify its own work, and other research groups would be needed alongside it.

A proposed policy and engineering framework, not existing law or scientific consensus.
