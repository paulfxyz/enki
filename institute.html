01 · The Institute

Research on what AI does to people — not just what it can do.

The proposed Institute would study AI's effects on work, wealth, culture and power, alongside the technical evidence needed for local-first deployment. Its research programme must test Enki's own assumptions as well as those of the industry.

A

Work & income

Study changes in tasks, wages and careers without equating exposure to AI with jobs already lost.

B

Culture & knowledge

How machine-made text, images and code reshape culture — and the trust built on it.

C

Power & markets

Who owns the compute, where the capex flows, and what that does to competition and sovereignty.

D

The open alternative

Evaluate open-weight models, licence restrictions, language coverage and deployment trade-offs. The current catalogue is not a completed validation programme.

Proposed roles: the Institute investigates; the Advisory applies the evidence

02 · The Advisory

Consultancy for the rooms where policy is written and capital moves.

A proposed advisory programme for public institutions and organisations. It would need qualified people, disclosed funding and conflict-of-interest rules before claiming independent advice or regulatory assurance.

For policy rooms

Parties, governments & public institutions

  • Local-first policy options, assessed against privacy, accessibility, security and service quality
  • An interoperable device-standard proposal, with documented conformance and security requirements
  • Reduce supplier dependence without pretending local hardware removes chip, software or energy dependencies
  • Proportionate access-control options for high-capability AI, subject to applicable law
  • Three proposed deployment tiers, with risk assessed by capability and use rather than size alone
  • Public procurement playbooks favouring open weights and local inference

For capital

Funds, family offices & industrial investors

  • Semiconductor and edge-inference theses beyond datacenter GPUs
  • Test infrastructure assumptions against workload-specific lifecycle and total-cost evidence
  • Market maps of the open-model economy, drawn straight from the registries
Why the split

Separate the roles. Keep society in control.

Our proposal combines enforceable oversight for consequential capabilities with useful local alternatives for everyday work. The boundaries need evidence: a small model can be misused, a remote system can be well governed, and not every task belongs on a phone. The Institute should measure those trade-offs and publish findings even when they challenge the manifesto.

Superintelligence — siloed and metered

A policy proposal for independent evaluation, incident reporting and tested containment. No technical disconnect can guarantee that earlier harm is reversible.

Everyday intelligence — everywhere, local

Local-first where capability, language quality, accessibility, security and cost meet the task. Coverage must be measured in real settings.
