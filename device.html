02 · The hardware

A datacenter in your pocket.

One smooth ceramic pebble that disappears into a pocket: silicon sized for open models, a WiFi mesh radio, one job — answer the intelligence your devices ask for, so the question never leaves home. No screen, no account, one brass button.

Concept — these are renders, not photographs. The device does not exist yet; designing and building it is precisely what the 300 are being assembled for.

Help build it

Six claims. One box.

01

Runs the models itself

Open models from the member-validated directory run on the box’s own silicon — the everyday tier of intelligence, on hardware you own.

02

Meshes with everything around it

Over WiFi it enrols the capable machines nearby — laptop, desktop, TV box — into one mesh, and routes each task to the chip that carries it best.

03

One standard port for every client

Everything is served as one localhost API/MCP endpoint — anything that accepts an endpoint can dock to it: no driver, no permission, no subscription.

04

Always carrying the best models

Whenever it meets a network it pulls cryptographically signed model updates — verified, logged, revocable — so it always runs the best small models the community has validated.

05

On. Off. That is the interface.

No screen, one button. A phone app handles pairing and updates in a minute — then the box disappears into your pocket and just answers.

06

Private by construction

Prompts, context and answers live on the box and your mesh — no account, no telemetry, no subscription. What you ask it is yours.

Outside your safe AI environments

The machines you own already form a pool of AI-capable silicon — a safe environment where intelligence can stay local. Step outside it and every question becomes a call to somebody’s cloud. The box closes that gap.

Distributed like a public good

Enki does not want to be the manufacturer. The blueprints ship open — hardware, firmware, models — for Apple, Samsung, Huawei, any maker, to mass-produce. Free where we can fund it, at a strict no-margin price where we can’t. The device is not a business; it is the mission’s delivery mechanism.

The 99%

The blueprint is the deliverable

Schematics, firmware, enclosure, model stack — published as an MIT-licensed open blueprint, free for Apple, Samsung, Huawei, any factory, any hobbyist, to build, adapt and mass-produce. Enki does not sell the device; it publishes the design: a portable AI datacenter, distributed to humanity, for the 99% of tasks intelligence is actually asked to do.

And the other 1%

Superintelligence, under lock

Frontier AI — and whatever follows it — belongs elsewhere: in hardened datacenters, behind real kill switches and real political guarantees. Licensed, inspected, accountable. Everyday intelligence in your pocket; superintelligence under lock. One demand, two halves: power, placed where people can see it.

EK1 — the reference build, engineered like we mean it.

A concept render is a promise; a reference build is a bill of materials. Here is how the pebble actually works — six design decisions, each one answerable, each one inherited from the ENKI-1 protocol the box exists to serve.

EK · 02Verified bootBoots only signed images. The device key lives in a secure element and signs the ENKI-1 device profile — one root of trust, hardware to mesh.

EK1 — the reference notes, in full

01 · Compute and memory

The box is built around one honest constraint: it must run the registry’s edge and laptop classes well, not the org class badly. That means NPU-class silicon with unified memory — the quantised weights, the KV cache and the vector index share one pool, because copying tensors across memory domains is where small machines go to die. The target is a box that holds a laptop-class model resident and answers at reading speed, with headroom for an embedding model beside it — the exact pair the Context Vault needs.

02 · Verified boot, one root of trust

The secure element holds the device key, generated on first boot, never exportable. That key does three jobs: it verifies the signed firmware image before anything runs, it signs the ENKI-1 device profile the box presents to the mesh, and it seals the owner’s data at rest. One root of trust from silicon to protocol — the box a stranger picks up is a ceramic paperweight.

03 · Thermal design is the spec

No fan, no vents, no dust, no noise — the ceramic shell itself is the radiator, and the sustained power budget is whatever it can shed in still air on a shelf. That number, not the benchmark peak, defines which models the box claims in its device profile. The throttle curve is tuned for the long middle of a conversation: steady tokens per second you can read along with, not a sprint that collapses after a minute.

04 · Radios and the mesh role

Bluetooth exists for one job: commissioning from a phone with a single confirmation — the Matter pattern, not a pairing ritual. WiFi carries everything else. On the mesh the box answers as an ENKI-1 gateway endpoint, advertises its capability document, and fetches model updates chunk by chunk from the nearest peers before touching the backbone. A street with five boxes updates mostly from itself.

05 · Nothing phones home

There is no account to create and no telemetry stream to anonymise, because the honest amount of telemetry for a privacy device is none. Updates are pulled on the owner’s schedule and verified against the signed registry snapshot — the box checks in with the commons, the commons never checks in on the box. The brass button is the physical layer of that promise: hold it and the radios cut, hardware-level, and the box keeps answering whatever is already on it.

06 · Repairable by right

The blueprint is MIT: schematics, board files, parts list, firmware — published, versioned, buildable by Apple or by a repair shop in Nairobi. Standard fasteners, no glue in the service path, storage on a socket so a dead flash chip is a part, not a funeral. Enki does not want to be the manufacturer; the reference build exists so that no manufacturer can make the open thing worse and call it compatible.

Status: reference design. EK1 is the engineering brief the 300 are being assembled to finish — targets and mechanisms above are the design contract, and every number lands in the open repository before a single unit is tooled.
