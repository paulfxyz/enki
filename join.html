    Voting runs as a DAO on a public blockchain — one member, one key, one vote, every tally publicly verifiable. Each ratified decision is published in plain words on our public GitHub.

    The annual audit

    A seat is held, never owned.

    Once a year, the 300 audit each other — secret DAO-signed ballots, citing the public record of contribution. The 10% judged to have contributed least leave with our thanks; 30 recruits step up. No seat is exempt — the founder’s least of all. Always, exactly, 300.

    Legal status

    Not registered yet — on purpose.

    Enki is deliberately not registered yet. Registering now would mean the founder picks the structure and everyone inherits it. Instead, once the 300 are complete, the DAO votes — one member, one ballot — on where Enki lives and what it legally is.

    Five structures shortlisted for the ballot

    • CHSwiss association (Verein) — the international default
    • FIFinnish association (ry) — the Nordic benchmark, e-voting in statute
    • EEEstonian non-profit (MTÜ) — the digital-native pick, run via e-Residency
    • SGSingapore CLG — Asia’s member-governed non-profit wrapper
    • USWyoming DUNA + 501(c)(3) — the DAO-native form, votes recognised on-chain

    The DAO, in plain words

    No tokens to buy, nothing to trade — the DAO is plumbing for fair, tamper-proof votes, not a speculative asset. Your member key is issued after ID verification, but the map between key and identity never touches the chain — it stays with the association, erasable on request. On-chain go only the ballot and the tally: enough to prove the vote was honest, never enough to name the voter. Vote on-chain, decision on GitHub — the record is permanent; your name is not part of it.

    The annual audit, in full

    Once a year, the whole 300 audit each other — the same DAO-signed ballots, pointed inward, citing the public record of contribution, not vibes. The 10% the members judge — by secret ballot, tally public, individual ballots never — to have contributed least leave with our thanks, and 30 new recruits step up from our pipeline of willing applicants. Same bar, same checks, full vote from day one. No seat is exempt — not even the founder's: if the members judge Paul Fleury among the 10%, he walks like anyone else. He doesn't even expect to stay: past the founding year, Paul plans to step back to an honorary founder's role — the 300 demand a depth of talent he is the first to say he may not match, year after year. A public good has no owner, and no ego worth protecting. The headcount never moves — thirty out, thirty in. Membership is always, exactly, the 300.

    The compensation rule, in full

    When a role eats real time — sometimes full time — Enki can compensate the member holding it. But whatever funding Enki receives, no one of the 300 — founder and secretary included — may draw more than 3× the median income of their country of residence. The only payments outside that cap are contractors and external services, and those sit outside the 300 entirely.

    Being one of the 300 is by no means a way to make big money. It covers your basic living costs, fairly inflated for the talent you bring to the society — nothing more. And the cap only limits what Enki pays: it never asks you to offset or give up your career, your business, or any other income you keep on the side.

    Five structures, one ballot

    1. CHSwiss association (Verein) — the default vehicle for international public-good bodies: legal personality the moment statutes are adopted, one member one vote by default, deep precedent from standards bodies to protocol foundations.
    2. FIFinnish registered association (ry) — the Nordic benchmark: a century-old Associations Act with one member, one vote written into statute, remote e-voting recognised by law, foreigners free to found and join.
    3. EEEstonian non-profit (MTÜ) — the digital-native pick: founded and governed fully online through e-Residency, member assembly supreme by law — and the register already holds its first internet-native non-profit.
    4. SGSingapore CLG — Asia's standard non-profit wrapper: a company limited by guarantee, no shareholders, member-governed, with a clear path to charity status.
    5. USWyoming DUNA + federal 501(c)(3) — the DAO-native form: on-chain votes recognised as governance, one hundred members minimum — the 300 qualify — paired with federal public-charity recognition for the mission.
